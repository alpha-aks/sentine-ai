\chapter{Literature Review}

\section{Evolution of Remote Invigilation Paradigms}

Systematic investigations into academic integrity have historically evaluated environmental deterrence, institutional honour codes, and the perceived probability of detection \cite{mccabe1993, watson2010}. In classical controlled classroom settings, physical invigilators enforce integrity through continuous spatial scanning and ambient acoustic monitoring. The transition to unproctored digital formats, however, introduced measurable score inflation and reduced grade variance across cohorts of identical academic standing \cite{daffin2018}, confirming that unsupervised remote contexts invite opportunistic dishonesty unless active countermeasures are deployed.

First-generation remote invigilation technologies relied primarily on periodic, asynchronous snapshot capture coupled with basic browser locking utilities \cite{viola2001}. These systems suffered from severe temporal blind spots: intermittent sampling rates (e.g., one image per 30--60 seconds) permitted candidates to consult unauthorized reference materials during inter-capture intervals without detection. Furthermore, coarse rule-based thresholds generated significant false alarms when confronted with environmental illumination variations or innocent postural shifts \cite{proctoru2020}.

Commercial platforms subsequently introduced proprietary deep learning classifiers to estimate aggregate suspicion metrics. However, independent audits have highlighted critical deficiencies regarding demographic bias in facial localization networks \cite{facialrecogbias2021}, excessive data collection practices conflicting with data sovereignty mandates \cite{eff2020}, and an absence of verifiable algorithmic transparency \cite{coghlan2021}.

\section{Visual Feature Extraction and Object Localization}

\subsection{Single-Stage Deep Object Detectors}

Real-time vision processing in remote examination environments requires high-throughput detection under strict hardware and latency budgets. The evolution of single-stage regression detectors, beginning with YOLOv1 \cite{yolo2016} and culminating in modern anchor-free architectures such as YOLOv8 and YOLOv11, superseded two-stage region-proposal paradigms (e.g., Faster R-CNN \cite{ren2015}) for edge and streaming tasks.

Single-stage architectures perform spatial bounding box regression and class confidence scoring in a unified forward pass. In the context of automated proctoring, target classes from canonical benchmarks such as MS-COCO \cite{lin2014} include \textit{person} (candidacy presence and multi-party intrusion), \textit{cell phone} (secondary communication devices), \textit{book} (unauthorized physical literature), and \textit{laptop} (secondary computing hardware). YOLOv11 integrates cross-stage partial spatial pyramid pooling modules, maintaining inference latencies below 70\,ms per frame on standard CPU targets.

\subsection{Facial Mesh and Landmark Topologies}

While generic bounding-box detectors confirm person presence, granular attentional analysis requires dense landmark tracking. MediaPipe Face Mesh \cite{mediapipe2020} parameterizes facial surfaces into 478 3D landmark coordinates in real time via lightweight convolutional sub-networks. This topological map enables direct geometric computation of ocular and nasal vectors without necessitating multi-gigabyte neural pipelines.

\section{Gaze Estimation and Attentional Modeling}

Attentiveness monitoring involves estimating whether a candidate's visual focus remains directed toward the active assessment display. Gaze estimation techniques diverge into appearance-based deep regression models and geometric landmark ratio approximations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Appearance-Based Models}: Architectures such as RT-GENE \cite{gazeML2018}, GazeTR \cite{cheng2022}, and L2CS-Net \cite{abdelrahman2023} learn continuous pitch and yaw mappings directly from eye and head crop tensors. While achieving angular errors below 4\textdegree, they incur substantial memory footprints and compute overheads that impede multi-stream server deployments.
    \item \textbf{Geometric Landmark Ratios}: Computational geometric approaches compute the spatial offset of the iris center relative to the inner and outer palpebral fissures (canthi) \cite{villanueva2007}. While sensitive to extreme head poses, landmark-ratio proxies execute in sub-5\,ms CPU cycles and provide sufficient discriminative power for coarse off-screen gaze classification.
\end{itemize}

\section{Acoustic Processing and Ambient Diarization}

Acoustic integrity monitoring entails detecting human speech events in the candidate's environment while filtering background ambient noise (e.g., HVAC units, outdoor vehicular traffic, mechanical keystroke chatter). 

Classical voice activity detection (VAD) relies on energy thresholding across spectral sub-bands \cite{webrtc2011}. Deep learning alternatives, such as Silero-VAD \cite{silerovad2021}, utilize recurrent neural units to produce frame-level speech probabilities across multilingual contexts. In examination proctoring, Noorbehbahani et al. \cite{noorbehbahani2022} demonstrated that sustained speech energy over rolling time windows strongly correlates with illicit peer communication.

\section{Behavioral Biometrics and Client Telemetry}

Non-visual telemetry provides a resilient secondary signal that is immune to camera occlusion. Key behavioral vectors include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Keystroke Dynamics}: Analysis of key dwell times and inter-key flight intervals serves as a continuous authentication vector, detecting sudden shifts in typing rhythm that may signal unauthorized physical substitution \cite{shen2020, teh2013}.
    \item \textbf{Clipboard Anomaly Quantification}: With the emergence of external generative assistants, unauthorized answer retrieval typically results in large, instantaneous text insertion events. Monitoring clipboard payload length distributions via statistical Z-score measures exposes discontinuous input bursts.
    \item \textbf{Operating System Context Transitions}: Capturing browser visibility state transitions (\texttt{visibilitychange}) and window focus degradation (\texttt{blur}) quantifies active application switching \cite{harmon2008}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Decision Fusion and Temporal Regularization}

Combining heterogeneous sensor streams requires a principled aggregation strategy. Classical ensemble theory identifies three primary fusion paradigms: fixed-rule voting/weighting, trained stacking meta-classifiers \cite{wolpert1992}, and Bayesian posterior updates \cite{tumer1996}.

For streaming proctoring systems, fixed-weight linear fusion combined with exponential moving average (EMA) smoothing provides predictable, deterministic scoring. Temporal regularization suppresses transient sensor noise (such as instantaneous camera auto-exposure adjustments or momentary blinks) from triggering spurious system alerts.

\section{Identified Research Gap}

The academic literature reveals a distinct gap: existing research prototypes focus on isolated single-modality perception tasks (e.g., gaze alone or keystrokes alone) without offering:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A unified, real-time microservices architecture that reconciles high-frequency video with discrete OS events under a sub-second latency envelope.
    \item Deterministic, natural-language explainable AI (XAI) rationale generation tailored for non-technical human invigilators.
    \item Cryptographically anchored post-examination audit receipts ensuring evidentiary integrity under regulatory frameworks such as the EU AI Act \cite{euaiact2024}.
\end{enumerate}

SentinelAI is engineered specifically to address this tripartite deficiency through an open, modular, and reproducible full-stack reference implementation.
